In 1948, sociologist and journalist **Alfred Winslow Jones** was assigned a story by his editor at _Fortune_ magazine to investigate popular stock market forecasting techniques. While interviewing Wall Street analysts, he realized market timing was a myth, but the assignment sparked the idea that created the world's first hedge fund.

**The Fortune Assignment**
- Jones was tasked with writing an article titled _"Fashions in Forecasting"_ to evaluate technical analysis and stock market predictors.
- He concluded that none of the market gurus could  predict market moves. 
- Rather than simply turning in the assignment, Jones realized he could remove broad market risk altogether by pairing long and short positions.

**The "Hedged" Strategy**

- **Market Neutrality:** Jones proposed buying (going long) fundamentally strong, undervalued stocks while simultaneously short selling overvalued, weak stocks.

**Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models”** by Alex Kim, Maximilian Muhn, and Valeri Nikolaev, its task is to estimate a company’s **future earnings direction**—whether earnings will rise or fall—not to forecast revenue, stock price, or total company performance directly. The researchers gave GPT‑4 anonymized, standardized financial statements and used chain-of-thought prompts that required trend analysis, ratio analysis, and a final earnings prediction.

In the reported experiment, GPT‑4 achieved about **60.31% directional accuracy**, compared with **52.71% for human analyst forecasts one month after the earnings release** and **60.45% for a specialized neural network**. The test used financial statements without narrative information such as management discussion or industry context.

The financial interpretation is therefore: GPT‑4 showed useful signal for **classifying next-year earnings changes**, and portfolios based on its predictions reportedly had stronger risk-adjusted performance than comparison strategies
