### <b>Introduction to Time Series</b>

<b>Time Series Definition</b>

A time series is a <b>stochastic process </b> indexed by time $t \in T \subseteq \R$.

Examples _(they are all continuous!):_
- $T = [0,1]$: Brownian Bridge from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$, where the pair notation denominates $(\text{time}, \text{value}) = (t, X_t)$
- $T = [0, + \infty]$: Brownian Motion starting at $t = (0,0)$
- $T = \R$: two sided Brownian Motion
- $T = \N_0$, or $T = \Z$: Discrete Random Walk.

We use the notation $\{X\}_{t \in T}$ to denote a random variable and the notation $\{x\}_{t \in T}$ to denote realizations of that variable.

A <b>time series model</b> is tiher the specification of:
-  all joint distributions $f(\{X\}_{t \in T})$ (i.e. the model provides $\Pr (X_{t1} \le x_{t1} ... X_{tk} \le x_{tk})$ for all $k \in \N$, $t_1...t_k$ and $x_1...x_k \in \R$
- all their means and covariances $(\mathbb{E}(X_t), \text{Cov}(X_t, X_p)) \forall t, p \in T$ 

<br/>
<b>White noise</b>

The i.i.d. noise ${X}_{t \in T}$ is a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables such that $\mathbb{E}(X_t) = 0 \forall t$. <br/>In general, we also assume that $\mathbb{E}[(X_t2)^2] = \text{Var}[X_t] < \infty \forall t$. _Note that in this case $\mathbb{E}[(X_t2)^2]$ does NOT depend on $t$._

<b>Rademacher Process</b>

Consider ${X}_{t \in T}$ such that $\Pr(X_t = - 1) = \Pr(X_t = 1) = 1/2 \forall t$.

If ${B_t}_t \sim_{iid} \text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, then:
- ${X_t}_t = 2 {B_t}_t - 1$
- $\text{Var}(X_t) = 4 \text{Var}(B_t) = 1 _(note that $X_t^2 = 1$ as $\mathbb{E}(X_t)^2 0 \text{Var}(X_t) = 1$)_ 

<b>White noise</b>

${X_t}_t$ is a sequence of centered and uncorrelated random variables. More specifically, $\mathbb{E}(X_t) = 0$, $\mathbb{E}(X_t^2) < \infty$ and $\text{Cov})(X_t, X_s) = 0 \forall t \neq s$.

- Note that i.i.i. noise such that $\mathbb{E}(X_t^2) < \infty$ is also a white noise.
- The term "white" is used by analogy to white light. Indeed, a white nnoise with costance variance $v^2$ admits a flat spectral density given by $f(\lambda) = v^2 / 2 \pi$, with $\lambda \in [-\pi, \pi] _(this means that all time-series frequencies are present)_.

<b>Random walk</b>

Let ${X_t}_t$ be a i.i.d. noise such that $\mathbb{E}(X_t^2) < \infty  \forall t \in \N$.

${S_t}_{t \in \N_0}$ is a random walkf if $S_0 = 0$ and $S_t = \sum_i^t X_i \forall t \ge 1$.


Id ${X_t}_{t \in \N_0}$ is a Rademacher process, then ${S_t}_{t \in \N_0}$ is called <b>symmetric random walk</b> with $\mathbb{E}(S_t) =\sum_i^t \mathbb{E}(X_i) = 0$.

<b>Time Series with trend</b>

Let ${Y_t}_t$ be a zero-mean time series and ${m_t}_t$ be a deterministic (non-random) sequence. \br Consider $X_t = Y_t + x_t, \forall t \in T$.

Them $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = m_T$ is called the <b>trend</b> of ${X_t}_t$.

<b>Time Series with seasonality</b>

Given a zero-mean time series $\{Y_t\}_t$ and $t \to p_t$ such that $p_t$ is periodic function with period $d$ known (i.e. $p_(t + d) = p_t$).

Consider the time series $X_t = Y_t + p_t \forall t \in T$. Then ${p_t}_t$ is called the <b>seasonal component</b> of ${Y_t}_t$.

For example, we could have the seasonal component in the following forms:

$$
p_t = a_0 + \sum_i^k (a_j \cos(\frac{2 \pi \alpha_j t}{d}) + b_j \sin(\frac{2 \pi \beta_j t }{d}))
$$

with known integers $k \ge 1, \alpha_j, \beta_j \ge > 0 $ and unkwown $a_0, a_j, b_j$, \forall 1 \le j \le k$.

<b>Time series with both trend and seasonakl component</b>

$$X_t = m_t + p_t + Y_t, \forall t \in T$$

with ${m_t}_t$ trend, ${p_t}_t$ the seasonal component with and ${Y_t}_t$ a zero-mean time series.

### <b>Stationarity and auto-covariance function (ACVF)</b>

<b>Stationarity</b>

A time series $\{X_t\}_t$ is sait do strictly (strongly) stationy if $\forall n \ge 1, h \in \Z$ it holds that:

$$
(X_1, ... X_n) =^d (X_{1+h},...X_{n+h})
$$

i.e. it means that th stochatic behavior of $\{X_t\}_t$ remains unchaged for _ANY_ time shift.

Let $\{X_t\}_t$ be a time series.

- The mean function of $\{X_t\}_t$ is defined as:

$$
\mu_x(t) \mathbb{E}(X_t), \forall t \in T
$$

- the covariance function of $\{X_t\}_t$ is defined as:
$$
\begin{align*}
\gamma_x (r,p) &= \text{Cov}(X_r, X_p) = \\
&= \mathbb{E}[(X_r - \mu_x(r))(X_p - \mu_x(p))] \\
&= \mathbb{E}[X_r X_p] - \mu_x(p) - \mu_x (r)
\end{align*}
$$ 

provided that $\mathbb{E[| X_r X_p|]} < \infty$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_r] < \infty \forall r,p$.

On the other handm, $\{X_t\}_t$ is said to be (weakly) stationary if $\mathbb{E}[X_t^2] < \infty \forall t and:
- $\mu_x(t)$ is costant in $t$
- $\gamma_x (t, t+h)$ depends only on $h$
