Challenging <b>"stocks for the long run"</b> means proposing an alternative, _even for retail investors with a long time horizon and low risk aversion,_ that goes beyond the <s>(simple)</s> _"100% stocks"_ approach. This is why, in <a href="https://giacomomaggiore.com/blog/16-model-portfolio" target="_blank">my latest blog posts</a>, I presented <b>my ideal asset allocation</b> as of October 2026 _(i.e. my model portfolio):_

<b>TL;DR:</b> the portfolio consists of the following ETFs

| ETF | Exposure | Weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| <a href="https://www.wisdomtree.com/it/products/equities/wisdomtree-global-efficient-core-ucits-etf---usd-acc" target="_blank">NTSG</a> | Global Equities + Global Bonds | 60% |
| <a href="https://www.direxion.com/product/auspice-broad-commodity-strategy-etf" target="_blank">COM</a> | Commodities with trend following | 10% |
| <a href="https://www.imgpfunds.com/funds/dbmf/" target="_blank">DBMF</a> | Managed Futures | 15% |
| <a href="https://www.wisdomtree.com/investments/etfs/capital-efficient/gde" target="_blank">GDE</a> | US Equities + Gold | 10% |
| <a href="https://www.cambriafunds.com/tail" target="_blank">TAIL</a> | Deep OTM options | 5% |

and the resulting exposure to asset classes/risk factors:

| Asset Class | Nominal exposure |
| --- | ---: |
| Stocks | 63% |
| Bonds | 36% |
| Commodities | 10% |
| Trend | 15% |
| Gold | 9% |
| Tail risk | 5% |
| <b>Total</b> | <b>138%</b>|

_(Note that the total is $> 100$ because of the implicit leverage from the futures contracts in <a href="https://www.wisdomtree.com/it/products/equities/wisdomtree-global-efficient-core-ucits-etf---usd-acc" target="_blank">NTSG</a> and <a href="https://www.wisdomtree.com/investments/etfs/capital-efficient/gde" target="_blank">GDE</a>. <a href="https://giacomomaggiore.com/blog/16-model-portfolio#riempire-il-piatto-il-pi-possibile" target="_blank">I briefly explained the underlying mechanism here</a>.)_

However, even though I agree with the total portfolio approach theory, there are still some aspects of this portfolio that do not convince me. Some can potentially be solved and come from translating theory $\to$ practice; others are <b>structural and unavoidable</b> for the underlying strategies.

## <b><s>Investing</s> to save pandas</b>

The first feature that bothers me is the <b>ESG filter</b> in NTSG, the core instrument of the portfolio and its long-term growth driver.

<a href="https://www.wisdomtree.com/api/product-documents/54028991-it-eu.product/SvYQ1JIhodwPcoEl3F3k8/download?locale=it-IT" target="_blank">The documentation says:</a>
<Quote>
The top 1500 companies by market capitalization that meet the above criteria are selected. Companies that do not meet the GSS [<b>Global Standards Screening (GSS)</b> WisdomTree will exclude companies that are “noncompliant” based on the ESG data providers _"GSS assessment"_] or are involved in certain business activities
such as Controversial Weapons, or are significantly involved in Thermal Coal, Tobacco,
Small Arms, and unconventional Oil & Gas are excluded from the eligible investment
universe
</Quote>

_Even though I support ESG criteria from an ideological and values-based perspective,_ there are many reasons why I see this approach as limited:
- companies are assessed on ESG parameters by third-party rating agencies that often use different methodologies and, _as a result,_ <a href="https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/26/6/1315/6590670" target="_blank">ratings from different providers diverge greatly</a> (correlation $\approx 0.54$; _for comparison:_ the same correlation for credit ratings is $\approx 0.99$). <br/>This gap is explained by different measurement criteria, definitions of sustainability and weights given to different aspects.
- <a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/eufm.12439" target="_blank">studies show</a> that there is <b>no</b> <b>statistically valid performance difference</b> between ESG and conventional funds.
- the rationale behind ESG investing is often to <b>"do good"</b>: that _by investing in "good" companies and not investing in "bad" ones (in other words, by raising the cost of money for the latter),_ one does good for the planet.<br/>However, first of all, <a href="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4359282" target="_blank">there is no broad evidence</a> that this reduction in capital affects management.<br/>Also, <a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4359282" target="_blank">studies suggest</a> that, _counterintuitively,_ reducing the cost of capital for _"green"_ companies brings very small marginal benefits to society while, _at the same time,_ making financing harder for _"non-green"_ companies <b>makes their impact on the world even worse.</b>

_As a result,_ the best way to <b>"boycott"</b> a non-green company is <b>not to buy its products</b>, not to base investments on third-party indicators. More broadly, the best way to make a positive impact on the world is to <b>change daily habits,</b> not tilt a portfolio in this direction.

## <b>Black Box</b>

The model portfolio presented is <b>far from transparent.</b><br/>For most stock/bond portfolios, _with some effort,_ it is possible to know which companies _(or sectors, countries, ratings, etc.)_ every euro of the portfolio is invested in.

_On the other hand,_ using instruments that 1) operate through futures and, _above all,_ 2) use <b>private systematic strategies</b> _(mainly based on trends)_ makes it much harder to get a snapshot of a portfolio's holdings.

For example, DBMF promises to use a systematic process to replicate the <b>performance of the main managed futures hedge funds</b> _without complexity, lock-ups and high costs,_ by identifying the main exposures, applying them through a proprietary model and taking exposure to liquid futures through weekly rebalancing.
<br/>Although the <a href="https://www.imgp.com/us/fund/US53700T8273-imgp-dbi-managed-futures-strategy-etf/#overview" target="_blank">product website</a> shows recent changes in the fund's holdings, the specific underlying quantitative strategies are clearly proprietary and, _as a result,_ not accessible to retail investors.

There is also a strong <b>model risk</b> component. First, there is no certainty about how managed-futures-based strategies "work" but, _more importantly,_ there is no guarantee that the ETF in the portfolio will be able to replicate these strategies in the future.

By contrast, COM's strategy is much clearer and, above all, simpler; the <a href="https://www.direxion.com/uploads/COM-Methodology.pdf" target="_blank">methodology</a> says:

<Quote>
The Index will either be long or in cash for each indivdual commodity based on a breakout in its price trend. It will be long
an individual commodity showing an upward price trend and flat (in cash) when a commodity is exhibiting a downward price
trend. The Index can be long anywhere ranging from 0-12 commodities based on each individual commodity’s price trend.
</Quote>

And, _in this case too,_ it is possible to see the holdings list transparently on a daily basis.

By also adding TAIL and GDE, it is still possible to run a <b>regression</b> to get an approximate estimate of exposure to the most common risk factors.

| Coefficient | Estimate | HAC p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alpha | 0.0006 | 0.4691 |
| VT excess | 0.5964 | &lt;0.0001 |
| BNDW excess | 0.3085 | 0.0001 |
| PDBC excess | 0.0130 | 0.4954 |
| WTMF excess | 0.1518 | 0.0002 |
| GLD excess | 0.1182 | &lt;0.0001 |


_Unsurprisingly,_ the model portfolio has statistically significant exposure to global bonds and equities, managed futures and gold. Commodity exposure is close to zero and <b>not significant</b>, _consistent_ with COM's trend-following strategy (and not a static long-only _beta_).

Running the same analysis on the famous Fama and French factors gives the following result.

| Coefficient | Estimate | HAC p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alpha | -0.0000 | 0.9983 |
| Market excess return | 0.6663 | &lt;0.0001 |
| SMB | -0.0629 | 0.2255 |
| HML | -0.0134 | 0.7897 |
| RMW | 0.0543 | 0.3106 |
| CMA | 0.0930 | 0.2415 |
| UMD | -0.0172 | 0.6610 |

<small><i>SMB: size factor, HML: value factor, RMW: profitability factor, CMA: investment factor, UMD: momentum</i></small>

The only statistically significant exposure is, _again,_ market beta. There are no persistent tilts towards size, value, profitability, investment or momentum.

## <b>The Second Law of Thermodynamics</b>

The third thing that does not convince me about the model portfolio is that <b>it is expensive.</b> The TERs of the instruments are:

| ETF | TER | 10-year TER impact on USD 100,000*|
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| NTSG | 0.25% | 2,728.87 USD |
| COM | 0.72% | 1,284.32 USD |
| DBMF | 0.85% | 2,261.99 USD |
| GDE | 0.20% | 364.61 USD |
| TAIL | 0.60% | 537.83 USD |

<small><i>*assuming a 7% gross annual return.</i></small>.

Together, these make up a portfolio with a <b>0.40% total TER</b> and much higher tracking error than a classic _"VT and chill."_

_Beyond the numbers themselves,_ I am not convinced by the <b>gap</b> between the ideal portfolio and the "real/investable" one. _At least in theory,_ everything seems to work well:
- weighted correlations and returns distributed according to the market regime
- capital efficiency thanks to the use of futures
- tail-risk protection through OTM options
- diversification based on risk factors rather than asset classes

_Etc. etc..._ yet, when it comes to opening the broker and clicking buy, the model portfolio runs into the <b>friction of reality</b> _(or the second law of thermodynamics of personal finance)_:
- ETFs with <b>high costs, low AUM, and low liquidity</b> _(Interactive Brokers took about ten seconds to execute my NTSG buy order... and I assure you I do not move millions)_
- <b>limited availability</b> of products to diversify issuer and model risk _(model risk)_
- relatively new products that do not allow statistically valid analysis, comparisons, and simulations

While the difference between the ideal and real version of a VT and chill portfolio is minimal _(a very low spread and an almost negligible TER),_ friction cannot be ignored for the model portfolio and, _in general,_ for all portfolios using more complex products and derivatives.
