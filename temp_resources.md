In this page I used to collect the list of all my favourite resources, _i.e. the podcasts, newsletters, YouTube channels and other content I consume on a daily basis._

However, _for a long time,_ this page has not been updated and I can't promise it will be in the future: _for most of the resources_ the <b>turnover is pretty high:</b> I easily fall in love with something and then leave it after a few months. 

_Still,_ I've always had some stable references that I've been consuming for a long time and that I still find valuable.

Therefore, the best way to get periodic inputs is to either 1) <a href="/newsletter" target="_blank">subscribe to the newsletter</a> _(approximately once per month, recently less)_ 2) read <a href="/blog" target="_blank">recent blog posts</a> 3) <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/giacomo-maggiore-499994263/" target="_blank">follow me on LinkedIn</a>.

Otherwise, always feel free to drop me a message on <a href="https://www.instagram.com/giacomomaggiore/" target="_blank">Instagram</a>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/giacomo-maggiore-499994263/" target="_blank">LinkedIn</a> or by <a mailto="giaco.maggiore@gmail.com">email</a>.

So... _depending on what you want to deep dive into, here are my stables._

<b>(Personal) Finance</b>

If you know me well enough, you probably know that I'm pretty passionate _(in Italy we say "in fissa")_ about these topics. If you are too, here you will find some generic, accessible, specific and technical resources I find valuable:

Italian 

- <a href="https://retireinprogress.com/" target="_blank">Mr. RIP</a> resources. If you are Italian, either 1) you know it or 2) you can start with <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@mr_rip">his videos and podcasts </a> on personal finance and investing. If you are not an Italian speaker, you can still enjoy his <a href="https://retireinprogress.com/" target="_blank">blog</a> or some of his <a href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLKuzEOMCClQU" target="_blank">English interviews</a> (I particularly liked this one with <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQNKUVrMKUI&t=3901s" target="_blank">Ben Felix</a> and the one about wealth management with <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Owm3FeDDjQQ&t=7098s" target="_blank">PWL Capital</a>).
- <a href="https://www.paolocoletti.com/" target="_blank">Paolo Coletti</a>'s resources. Again, if you are Italian, either 1) you recognize his voice or 2) you should start the series <a href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLfuzpc-H8qcdyEb5rpgYhN2P7cJGR1i9h" target="_blank">Educati e Finanziati</a> on the basics of money management. Otherwise, there's nothing to do.
- <a href="https://podcasts.apple.com/it/podcast/the-bull-il-tuo-podcast-di-finanza-personale/id1692088632" target="_blank">The Bull Podcast</a> by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/riccardo-spada-mba-26723631/" target="_blank">Riccardo Spada</a>. Two short episodes a week covering everything happening in the markets, retail investing topics and interviews with financial professionals. Also recommend the newsletter for financial news and analysis.
- <a href="https://www.toobigtofail.it/" target="_blank">Too Big to Fail</a> Podcast. Probably the most "funny" resource in this list. I would not classify it as "beginner friendly" for the content, but the hosts speak to you like likeable friends their mother doesn't want you to see.
- <a href="https://www.dedaloinvest.com/" target="_blank">Dedalo Invest</a>. One of the most valuable and big blogs around, covering portfolio allocation, behavioral finance and financial history.
- <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/alessandropedone/" target="_blank">Alessandro Pedone</a>'s projects and resources. Long-term financial advisor and co-founder of <a href="https://omney.it/" target="_blank">Omney</a> with <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@riccardozanetti" target="_blank">Riccardo Zanetti</a>, he often provides his expertise on the financial industry and why retail investing is not just about <a href="https://www.vanguard.com/ie/en/products/etf/equity/9679/vanguard-ftse-all-world-ucits-etf-usd-accl" target="_blank">VWCE</a> and chill.


English 

- <a href="https://www.bankeronwheels.com/" target="_blank">Banker on Wheels</a>. Probably one of the best international websites for retail investors, collecting articles and analyses from different resources split by topic. He's also a cyclist, so +1 point.
- <a href="https://awealthofcommonsense.com/" target="_blank">A Wealth of Common Sense</a> by <a href="https://awealthofcommonsense.com/about/" target="_blank">Ben Carlson</a>: short and easy-going blog posts, perhaps a little too simple and US-biased, but often providing useful data and charts.
- <a href="https://ofdollarsanddata.com/" target="_blank">Of Dollars and Data</a> by <a href="https://ofdollarsanddata.com/about/" target="_blank">Nick Maggiulli</a>. If you are looking for data-driven analysis covering both personal finance and lifestyle/philosophy, it is a good starting point (also recommend his book <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wealth-Ladder-Nick-Maggiulli/dp/0593976661" target="_blank">The Wealth Ladder</a>).
- <a href="https://theitalianleathersofa.com/" target="_blank">The Italian Leather Sofa</a>. Italian writer and expat in Zurich, writing in English non-beginner-friendly blog posts on leverage and _truly_ diversified strategies (not the classical 60/40).
- <a href="https://rationalreminder.libsyn.com/" target="_blank">The Rational Reminder Podcast</a> by <a href="https://common-sense-investing.ca/" target="_blank">Ben Felix</a>. Even if you are not Canadian, there's still value there.
- <a href="https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/" target="_blank">Aswath Damodaran</a>'s website and blog. Not directly related to my main interests, but sometimes it's good to remember ourselves what is behind the stock market: balance sheets and company valuation.
- <a href="https://portfoliocharts.com/" target="_blank">Portfolio Charts</a>' blog. Maybe you've used some of the tools to optimize or backtest your portfolio; it also writes good stories around the topic.

<b>STEM, Tech and related</b>

Ok... I'm studying economics and working in finance, but my engineering roots _(thanks, <a href="https://www.polimi.it/" target="_blank">Politecnico di Milano</a>!)_ are still alive:

- <a href="https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=salvatore+sanfilippo" target="_blank">Salvatore Sanfilippo</a>, aka <a href="https://www.antirez.com/" target="_blank">Antirez</a>, long-term software engineer, founder of <a href="https://redis.io/" target="_blank">Redis</a> and recent YouTuber/influencer/sex symbol. He has recently posted too many YouTube videos (Italian only). Some are funny, some are valuable, and some are a good way to view ads. Otherwise, his not-so-active blog contains useful reflections (English only).
- <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@Technicismi" target="_blank">Technicismi</a> by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/riccardobassetto/" target="_blank">Riccardo Bassetto</a> and <a href="https://www.willmedia.it/actually" target="_blank">Actually</a> by <a href="https://willmedia.it/" target="_blank">Will Media</a>. Everything that happens weekly around the world with a tech, entrepreneurship and politics focus.
- <a href="https://josvisser.substack.com/" target="_blank">Wednesday Wisdom</a> by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/josvisser/" target="_blank">Jos Visser</a>. Life and work lessons from an old-school software engineer at <a href="https://www.google.com/" target="_blank">Google</a> (if you are an engineer, I definitely recommend <a href="https://josvisser.substack.com/p/all-communication-is-strategic?utm_source=publication-search" target="_blank">All Communication is Strategic</a>).
- <a href="https://ascensore.media/" target="_blank">Ascensore Media</a>, basically the Italian version of <a href="https://abc.com/shows/shark-tank" target="_blank">Shark Tank</a>, less boomer. Nothing else: startups pitch to compete for zero euros in investment rounds.


<b>Everything else</b>

I do not have a bucket for everything so here a bunch of stuff I enjoy:

- <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@francesco_puppi" target="_blank">Francesco Puppi</a>'s YouTube, blog and podcast. If you are an Italian trail runner, you probably know him. If you are an Italian runner, you should get to know him.
- <a href="https://luca-dellanna.com/" target="_blank">Luca Dell'Anna</a>'s blog posts and newsletter. What I like: risk and work lessons anyone can apply in their context. What I don't like: the posts are too generic and short (also recommend his book <a href="https://luca-dellanna.com/ergodicity-economics/" target="_blank">Ergodicity</a>).
- <a href="https://ergodicityeconomics.com/" target="_blank">Ergodicity Economics</a>, from the <a href="https://www.lml.org.uk/" target="_blank">London Mathematical Laboratory</a>. An independent research group focused on applying the concept of ergodicity in different areas (mostly finance and economics) with a scientific and rigorous approach. Not beginner friendly and somewhat technical.
- <a href="https://www.ilpost.it/" target="_blank">Il Post</a> (Italian only). I do not talk about news or politics often, but I'm a huge fan and supporter of this newspaper (and its founder <a href="https://www.francescocosta.net/" target="_blank">Francesco Costa</a> too; if you are interested in the US, check his books).
- <a href="https://www.nassimnicholastaleb.org/" target="_blank">Nassim Nicholas Taleb</a>'s newsletter and books. He can be polarizing and sometimes hard to understand (I'm still sure I didn't get 100% of his books), but he is one of the most intelligent thinkers today, in my view.
- <a href="https://thelogicofrisk.com/" target="_blank">The Logic of Risk</a>, from <a href="https://pasqualecirillo.com/" target="_blank">Pasquale Cirillo</a>. Anything from risk management, extreme value theory, probability and applications in economics and finance.
- <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@JohnFish" target="_blank">John Fish</a>. Probably he doesn't have much of an online presence anymore, but back in the day (approximately 5–6 years ago) he was a great inspiration for anyone in college who was passionate about reading and slow living.
- <a href="https://calnewport.com/" target="_blank">Cal Newport</a>'s blog, podcast and books. If you find it difficult to work or focus on long-term tasks, check <a href="https://www.calnewport.com/books/deep-work/" target="_blank">Deep Work</a> and <a href="https://www.calnewport.com/books/slow-productivity/" target="_blank">Slow Productivity</a>. There are good, accessible resources on his website too.

Cheers!
